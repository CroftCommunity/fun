The play surface — the board fills the stage, the door opens, one shape for hand controls

Ten proposals for every game page, to be talked through before the plan is phased: the two systemic defects the owner's 2026-09-04 screenshots showed (Play off the bottom of a poster; a tiny board in an empty stage) are built on the branch and captured, so Proposed is pixels for those; the rest — a ground per game, one vocabulary for hand controls, Furrow standing upright, life on the versus boards, pieces with character, Loose Ends' curve, two music picks — are sketches, labelled, for choosing a direction. Plan: plans/2026-09-04-plan-play-surface.md (draft; phases follow the decisions below).

Compare in one skin: every capture and sketch here is the default Worlds night skin. Captures are true size — 390×844 as-is, 1280×900 shown in an 1180×740 window, top-aligned — from tools/mock-snaps.mjs all --out f-play-surface --tag current|proposed, hermetic: fixed seeds, and the poster is the bare land in a fresh context.

The proposals, Current beside Proposed

1 · The door opens — Play is reachable on every poster built · F1.1, F1.2

The poster's body becomes the scroller and Play sticks to its foot. A setup card taller than the frame scrolls under a Play that never leaves the screen; keyboard focus lands an option above it (scroll-padding). Every game gets this; nothing else on the poster changes.

2 · The board fills the stage built · F2.1–F2.5

The stage becomes a size container and declares the room a board may fill — its width, and its height less a 3rem lane at the foot for the toast. Each grid takes its cell from the stage's short side instead of the viewport's width: Othello, checkers, chess, Drop 4, Dots, 2048, Wyrdle, Blockdoku; Color Sort's tubes grow with the stage's height on a desktop and keep mock E's 48px on a phone; the Orchard crate caps against the stage and — with the conflict marker gone — sits in the middle. Floors keep every 360px rule; ceilings keep a disc a disc. The spec asserts ≥80% of one axis on a desktop, ≥85% of the width on a phone, and never past the stage.

Not yet on the rule, and each a phase of its own with the same spec shape: Furrow (proposal 6 first), Bubble (its canvas already flexes; the tap floor is the check), Align's well, cribbage's table, Mahjong's layout, Loose Ends (its canvas already fills), solitaire and Trio Tumble. The checkers and chess captures are the same shape as Othello's and are in snaps/f-play-surface/.

3 · The stage's transients stay out of the way built · F2.5, F3.1

A toast wraps inside the stage instead of running off a phone, and a full-height board leaves it a lane — the two captures under proposal 2 (Othello desktop, 2048 phone) show both. Nothing to decide; recorded because it fell out of the fill.

4 · A ground per game sketch · Q2

Wyrdle's "so much negative space and it's just blah". The frame keeps its gallery black; each game sets one hook — a low-alpha radial tint of its own board colour with a vignette — so the room around the board reads as that game's room, not a void. Chrome roles stay skinnable; the tint is game-owned, like the board (ADR-0001). Drawn on Wyrdle and 2048 (the 2048 sketch also shows Q3's pad off).

5 · One shape for hand controls — gestures first, one pad, Align split sketch · Q3, Q4

Today: 2048 has a d-pad of four 48px buttons that is always there; Align has seven buttons in three rows under the well, each a different width, none branded. Proposed: one shared pad component the frame draws over the stage's ground — rounded 64px targets tinted with the game's colour, translucent, haptic on press — in two layouts: a d-pad (2048) and a two-thumb split (Align: move under the left thumb, rotate and drop under the right, Hold and Next beside the well). A preference, On-screen controls: Auto / On / Off, governs both games; Auto shows the pad on a coarse pointer only. Swipes stay: 2048's board already reads them; Align gains swipe-down for hard drop and tap-to-rotate on the well.

6 · Furrow stands up on a phone sketch · Q5

A mancala row cannot wrap, so across a phone it scrolls and the stores clip. Upright, the same fourteen cells are two columns of six with a store at each end — the engine's column on the left sowing downward, yours on the right sowing upward, the same counter-clockwise circuit turned a quarter. The pits grow to 44px+ from 2.75rem. Board: Auto / Across / Upright in Settings; Auto follows the stage's aspect, so a desktop stays across.

7 · Life on the versus boards sketch · Q11

"Drop 4 … lackluster gameplay", "checkers gameplay could use some pick me up", "cribbage needs more animations and life". One vocabulary of beats, shared by the six versus games and cribbage, each a Web Animations API call the game triggers from the move the core already resolved (the same rule as Color Sort's pour): the drop (Drop 4's disc falls with a trail and settles with a bounce; Othello's flips run outward in sequence; a checkers piece slides its chain and a captured piece shrinks out; a chess piece slides), the reply mark (the engine's move ringed for a beat — exists), the line (a winning four, a closed box, a flipped run pulses), the flourish (the score in the seat ticks up; one word in the game's display face — "Four!", "King!", "Fifteen two" — that fades), and the result screen's fanfare (exists). Sound has a voice per beat under the Sound toggle; reduced motion collapses each beat to its last frame. Nothing fires on an ordinary move.

8 · Pieces with character — Bubble as emoji, chess with presence sketch · Q7, Q8

"Bubbles looks pretty cheesy … maybe we use emojis as pieces." Six colours become six fruit — each still a distinct shape, so the colour-blind pairing the tokens test grades is kept — inside glassy bubbles, fired from a launcher that shows the loaded and the next piece; the aim line is a dotted arc. Chess: the pieces stay glyphs but get a pack row in Settings (Classic, Bold; Emoji later), and the board gets a wooden frame, coordinates outside the squares, a shadow on the ground, and a last-move ring — the "life and presence" of a real set.

9 · Loose Ends starts as a puzzle sketch · Q10

What the genre's players say they enjoy is reading the knot: a board that looks impossible until the order reveals itself, difficulty rising through density and interleaving, never through a clock (the sources are in the plan). Level 1 today has three arrows and nothing to read. Proposed curve: level 1 = 10 arrows on 6×8, snakes of 3–5; Easy ends (level 15) at ~24 arrows on 8×11; the top of the curve stays where it is (68 on 18×26). The core's generator already places arrows in reverse solution order, so density is a config change with golden vectors re-recorded; a level's star already rewards a clean solve. Parked for a research pass: a second mechanic — colour bands that hide the flow, locked arrows that need a neighbour freed first, a par with three stars.

10 · Two music picks decision · Q9

Mahjong names no track in src/music.ts, so it plays the shelf's bed, Morning Miles; Cribbage names Morning Miles outright. Both are one line each once the owner picks from the airier pieces. Nothing to draw.

Does the build match the mock?

Proposals 1–3 are already held by tests/play-surface.spec.ts — F1.1, F1.2 (the door), F2.1–F2.5 (the fill, the tube, the crate, the lane), F3.1 (the toast) — titled the way a claims file names them, on both engines. The claims file (f-play-surface.claims.json) and the plan's phase gating (tests/mock-parity.test.ts) come with the plan's Pass 3, once Q1–Q11 are answered and the sketches become promises. A sketch carries no claim.
