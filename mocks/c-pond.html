Direction C · quiet chrome · shelf structure

The Pond

The opposite bet from B. Instead of matching the art's energy, the chrome goes completely quiet — warm paper, hairline rules, a serif, a lot of air — so each piece of art is the only loud thing on screen. The home page is a browsable index where the tier is a printed shelf label with a sentence saying what it promises, because "you can re-check the result yourself" is this shelf's real differentiator and no icon can say it.

What this direction is betting on

IdentityWarm paper, hairline rules, one umber accent, a serif for display. The chrome contributes no colour at all.
Home pageA shelf. Each tier gets a printed label and a sentence saying what it promises, so "provable" is argued rather than badged.
ArtUsed sparingly and large — one plate per screenful, small precise tiles in the index. The restraint is what makes the art land.
BoardsRendered with less ink. Othello becomes a paper board; Trio Tumble drops the glow. Deliberately quieter than the art that sells it.
The riskIt may read as too calm for a games site — and it puts the chrome in tension with the art it is framing. Judge it against B side by side.
