# Mancala — the fifth adversarial game

status: **Pass 1 + Pass 2 complete.** Executing: Phase 0 done, Phase 1 next.

**owner decisions (2026-08-07):** **6 pits × 4 seeds** — the recognizable game,
with the honest-Oracle shape the shelf already has two precedents for — and the
shelf name **Furrow**, a crofting word for the shelf's own idiom. Two players
only, and the full §10 checklist.

## Problem Statement

The shelf has four adversarial games — Drop 4, Othello, checkers, Dots and Boxes
— and a shared stack behind them: `adversary_core::Adversary`, `adversary_solver`'s
class-preserving band + `NodeBudget`, the tutor's `{quality, exact}` interface,
`hybrid-player.ts`, and the game-agnostic scoring rig behind `GameOracle`.

Dots (2026-08-07) was the first game built to *use* that stack rather than to
prove it, and it needed no change to any shared file. The open question it leaves
is whether that was because the stack is general or because dots happened to be
easy: its move was a bare index, its board was 24 bits, and its whole game was
solved from four plies in.

Mancala is the next game from discovery's catalog family (`p2p-games-pond-launch-set.md`
names it in the same build-fresh vein as backgammon and Nine Men's Morris) and it
tests the stack in **four** places none of the four shipped games do:

1. **One move mutates many cells.** Sowing takes every seed out of one pit and
   drops them one at a time around the board — up to thirteen writes from a single
   move code. Every shipped game's move touches one or two cells (checkers' jump
   chain is the outlier, and it is still a path of squares). This is the first
   game where *replay correctness depends on a loop*, which is exactly the class
   of bug a state hash exists to catch.
2. **A terminal that transforms the score.** When one side has no seeds, the
   opponent **sweeps** every remaining seed into their store. The final score is
   therefore not what accumulated during play; a terminal rule rewrites it. No
   shipped game has an end-of-game transformation.
3. **All three result classes are reachable.** Dots introduced a margin-valued
   band, but its draw arm was unreachable (nine boxes cannot split) and existed
   only for a free function to test. Here an even seed count makes a draw a real
   outcome, so `class_of` is a three-way sign function with every arm live.
4. **The extra turn is inherited, not introduced.** Landing your last seed in your
   own store gives you another move — the rule dots proved the trait, the runner
   and the scorer already carry. This is the first chance to confirm a shape
   *transfers* rather than to prove it once. If it needs any change here, the dots
   result was luck.

## Reasoning

### The variant, and what it costs to pick the famous one

The plan is written for **Kalah**, the variant most people mean by "mancala": two
rows of pits, one store per player, sow counterclockwise, extra turn on your
store, capture on landing in your own empty pit.

The size is the real decision, and it is the same one dots faced:

| | 6 pits × 4 seeds (48 seeds) | a small board (e.g. 4×3 or 3×3) |
|---|---|---|
| recognizable | **yes — this is "mancala"** | reads as a toy |
| exactly solvable **in a browser** | almost certainly not from the opening | plausibly yes |
| honest top level | `Expert` (deepest search) | `Perfect` |
| tutor's wording | hedges until the endgame | proves nearly everywhere |
| game length | a few minutes | under a minute |

Dots chose small deliberately, so its tutor would not hedge for the whole game.
**The recommendation here is the opposite: take 6 × 4.** Three reasons:

- The shelf already has **two** unsolved-game precedents (Othello's endgame-solve
  variant, checkers' proof-of-terminal variant) and `docs/AI-PLAYERS.md` records
  both. It does not need a third demonstration that hedging works; it needs the
  game people recognize.
- Mancala has a property that makes the tractability threshold unusually clean:
  **seeds in play only ever decrease.** A seed that enters a store never leaves
  it. So "seeds still outside the stores" is a *monotone non-increasing* measure of
  how much game is left, which is exactly the shape Othello's `TRACTABLE_EMPTIES`
  relies on and exactly what checkers lacks (its positions cycle, which is why its
  honesty flag had to mean "a terminal was proven" instead). The endgame will fall
  to an exact solve; Phase 0 measures where.
- The literature reports Kalah(6,4) as a first-player win. **This plan does not
  assert that**, and Phase 0 does not assume it: it measures our own value under
  our own rules, and a disagreement means our rules differ from the published
  ones, which is a finding worth having before the tutor claims anything.

### Why the value is a seed margin, and where the honesty flag turns on

Same shape dots established:

```
negamax(pos) -> the best future seed margin for the side to move
  for each non-empty pit p on the mover's side:
      next = sow(pos, p)                       // one move, many writes
      if next.to_move == pos.to_move:          // landed in own store
          v =  negamax(next)                   // the turn was kept: same perspective
      else:
          v = -negamax(next)                   // the turn passed: flip
  terminal -> sweep, then (mover_store - opponent_store)
```

The per-move value the band and tutor consume is the **final** margin from the
mover's perspective, and its class is the sign. `exact` is true iff the search
that produced it reached terminals rather than a horizon — never derived from the
position.

Unlike dots, the memo cannot be a flat array: the position is fourteen counts
summing to 48, not a bitmask, so this uses a **`HashMap` transposition table with
bounds**, which is checkers' pattern (`crates/checkers-solver/src/search.rs`) and
carries checkers' warning with it — a TT entry must respect the window it was
stored under, or the "exact" it reports is a lie about a bound.

### What Phase 0 has to answer before the solver is designed

This repo's most expensive recent lesson (P9, and again in dots' Phase 3) is that
predictions about search cost here are wrong more often than right. So Phase 0
measures, and the plan below deliberately commits to no constant:

- **Where is the exact-solve knee**, as a function of seeds in play? That number
  becomes `TRACTABLE_SEEDS`, and it is *not* a speed knob afterwards.
- **What is the real branching factor and game length?** Six pits is the upper
  bound on moves, but empty pits and extra-turn chains change both.
- **Does the budget bite?** If it does, iterative deepening plausibly pays here
  where it did not in dots or checkers — `deepen` was +14% on checkers, −41% on
  Othello, and inapplicable in dots. Mancala is the first game on the shelf with a
  genuinely deep midgame and a small branching factor, which is deepening's
  natural home. Phase 12 decides it on numbers either way.
- **Does our value agree with the published one?** A cross-check, in the same
  spirit as dots checking its solver against an independently written spike.

### Alternatives considered and rejected

- **Wrap an existing implementation (Tier 2).** No candidate on the discovery
  catalogs is a packaged mancala clearing the inclusion filter, and the rules are
  far simpler than an integration — the exact condition for build-fresh.
- **Ultimate Tic-Tac-Toe first.** Cheaper, and it stresses a different thing
  (legal moves constrained by the opponent's previous move). Considered and set
  aside on 2026-08-07: it does not exercise the many-cell move or the terminal
  transformation, which are the two shapes worth learning next. It stays on the
  slate.
- **A size picker.** Rejected for the same reason dots rejected one: it multiplies
  the tractability threshold, the band tuning and the golden vectors by the number
  of sizes for no proof one size does not already give. `PITS`/`SEEDS` go into the
  state hash as little-endian `u32` (the Drop 4 and dots precedent), so adding a
  size later is additive and re-locks no recorded vector.
- **Modelling the extra turn as a null move for the opponent.** Rejected for dots
  and rejected again: it would put a move in the verifiable record that nobody
  made.
- **Avoiding the sweep by ending the game when a side empties.** Some casual
  implementations do this. Rejected — it changes who wins, and the sweep is the
  standard rule; the terminal transformation is also one of the four reasons to
  build this game.

## Verified Assumptions

Each confirmed by reading the named file this session, not inferred.

- **`Adversary` does not assume alternation, and the extra turn needs nothing new.**
  `side_to_move(pos: &Self::Position) -> Side` is state-derived
  (`crates/adversary-core/src/lib.rs`, the trait body). Dots proved this end to end
  on 2026-08-07; the crate's header prose was corrected then and now says so.
- **The scoring rig does not assume alternation.** `runMatch` picks the player from
  the live board every iteration and `gradeSide` re-derives whose move it is during
  replay (`src/harness/match-runner.ts`, `src/harness/scorer.ts`). Verified by dots
  grading with **zero** rig edits.
- **A move is a plain number on the port.** `GameOracle.play(code: number)` /
  `legalMoves(): number[]` (`src/harness/game-oracle.ts`). A pit index needs no
  packing — this will be the second-thinnest adapter on the shelf after dots'.
- **`select_in_band` is generic over the move type and deliberately excludes
  `capped_class` and `live_band`** as per-game judgement
  (`crates/adversary-solver/src/lib.rs:18-27`). A seed margin is a legitimate
  instance, exactly as a box margin was.
- **`NodeBudget` is nodes, never a clock**, because the baselines assert exact
  Reports and the wasm has no host time import (`crates/adversary-solver/src/lib.rs`).
- **The transposition-table pattern already exists and carries its own warning.**
  `crates/checkers-solver/src/search.rs` has `Table` / `TtKey` / `TtEntry` with a
  `Bound` enum and a `proven` flag, plus `Table::disabled()` — the control that
  lets a test assert the table changes *speed and not results*. Reuse the shape,
  including that control.
- **`deepen` exists and its payoff is game-specific**, measured at +14% (checkers,
  reverted), −41% (Othello, shipped) and inapplicable (dots)
  (`crates/adversary-solver/src/lib.rs`, `crates/othello-solver/src/search.rs`,
  `docs/AI-PLAYERS.md` → "When iterative deepening pays").
- **`pond_outcome::Game` is `replay(seed, moves) -> Replayed`** with `final_hash`
  and `won` (`crates/pond-outcome/src/lib.rs:18-41`). `Replayed::won` means Side A
  won, as it does in the other four.
- **The wiring points for a new game are five files**: `src/registry.ts`,
  `src/how-to-registry.ts`, `GAME_PAGES` + the wasm copy step in `build.mjs`, the
  `-p <game>-wasm` list in `tools/build-wasm.sh`, and `crates/xbuild` for the
  native==wasm check. Verified by touching every one of them for dots on
  2026-08-07.

**Resolved by Phase 0** (findings below): the cost of an exact solve at each seed
count; the branching factor and game length; and the opening's solvability, which
is *not* reachable and therefore settles the top level as `Expert`.

**Still unverified after Phase 0:** whether alpha-beta moves the threshold past
16 seeds (Phase 3), and whether iterative deepening pays for the **capped**
search — Phase 0 measured the exact path's truncation rate, which is a different
quantity (Phase 12).

### Phase 0 findings (measured 2026-08-07/08, `spike/mancala-solve/`)

Machine: darwin/arm64, release build, memoized negamax with **no alpha-beta** —
so every "nodes" figure below is the count of **distinct reachable positions**
below that root, which is a floor any enumerating search must pay and which
alpha-beta can beat by pruning. Read them as an upper bound on the shipped
solver's cost, not as its cost.

**The rules were verified before any number was trusted.** Conservation over 2,000
random games, the classic pit-2 opening keeping the turn, the opponent's store
being skipped on a lap, capture, no-capture-when-the-opposite-is-empty, and a
hand-derived sweep. One check failed, and it was **the expectation that was
wrong, not the code**: a 13-seed lap from pit 5 lands back in pit 5, which the
lift emptied, so it captures. Kalah has no self-capture exception. That is now
pinned as its own case, because it is exactly the rule a re-implementation would
get wrong quietly.

#### 1. The tractability knee is sharp, and it is between 16 and 18 seeds

| seeds in play | distinct positions | worst of 5 |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 2 | 0.0 ms |
| 6 | 63 | 0.0 ms |
| 8 | 2,972 | 0.3 ms |
| 10 | 31,026 | 3.9 ms |
| 12 | 337,596 | 49.5 ms |
| 14 | 740,022 | 122.3 ms |
| **16** | **1,648,032** | **315.0 ms** |
| 18 | 11,160,700 | 3,537 ms |
| 20 | 30,203,568 | 19,486 ms |
| 24 | 164,362,385 | 230,942 ms (3.8 min) |

16 → 18 is a **6.8× jump in positions and 11× in time**, against ~2.2× for the
step before it. `TRACTABLE_SEEDS = 16` is the starting point; Phase 3 re-measures
once alpha-beta exists, because pruning may buy 18.

**The finding that changes Phase 3's design: the constraint is memory, not time.**
1.65M distinct positions at the threshold means a memo of 1.65M live entries.
A `HashMap<[u8; 13], i32>` at that size is tens of megabytes in a browser tab —
dots got away with a flat 16.7 MB array because its key was a 24-bit mask, and
this key is not. Phase 3 should **pack the twelve pit counts plus the side into a
`u64`** (6 bits each is 73 bits — so pack the counts as 5 bits with an overflow
escape, or key on a 64-bit hash with verification) **and use a fixed-size
open-addressing table**, not `HashMap`. Sizing that table is a Phase 3 decision
with this number behind it.

#### 2. The game is short, narrow, and mostly single-move turns

Over 200 random-legal-move self-play games:

- **47.2 moves per game** (max 77) — the shelf's one-minute tempo holds.
- **branching factor 4.11** (max 6) — narrow, which is the shape that makes deep
  search affordable and makes move ordering worth having.
- **1.17 moves per turn** (max **5**) — extra-turn chains are real but usually
  short. The UI must handle a five-move turn; it will mostly render one.

#### 3. The opening does not solve, and the honest top level is `Expert`

| budget | outcome |
|---|---|
| 10,000,000 nodes | exhausted, 7.0 s |
| 100,000,000 nodes | exhausted, 498 s |

Not solved, and **the cost is worse than linear**: 10× the nodes cost 71× the
time, because the memo stops fitting cache long before it stops fitting memory. A
third attempt at 800M was abandoned rather than run — it would have needed a
multi-gigabyte live table for an answer these two already give.

So this game ships with **`Expert` as its top level, not `Perfect`** — the Othello
and checkers shape, which the plan predicted and the shelf already documents. The
literature's reported first-player win for Kalah(6,4) was **not reproduced here
and is not relied on anywhere**; that solve used retrograde analysis and endgame
databases, which is a different technique from anything this game will ship.

#### 4. Roughly 70% of a game is above the exact threshold

How often a fixed allowance truncates an exact solve attempt, over 12 games:

| budget | truncated |
|---|---|
| 200,000 nodes | 82.0% (446/544) |
| 1,000,000 nodes | 76.5% (416/544) |
| 4,000,000 nodes | 71.3% (388/544) |

Two things follow. **Spending more does not buy much**: a 20× budget moves the
rate 82% → 71%, because the state space above ~18 seeds is out of reach at any
budget a browser can spend. And **the heuristic carries about 70% of the game**,
which predicts the harness's graded fraction lands near **30%** — between
checkers' 5% and dots' 83%, as the plan expected.

**This does not settle the deepening question.** It measures how often an *exact*
attempt fails, which is mostly a restatement of where the threshold sits. The
number `docs/AI-PLAYERS.md` names as deepening's precondition is the bite rate of
the **capped** search's budget, and that search does not exist yet. Phase 12
measures it. The prior is still that deepening pays here — narrow branching, deep
midgame — but a prior is not a measurement.

#### What this changes in the plan

- `TRACTABLE_SEEDS` starts at **16**, re-measured in Phase 3 with alpha-beta.
- Phase 3 gains an explicit memo-representation decision: **a packed key and a
  fixed-size table**, not `HashMap`, and sized against 1.65M entries.
- Phase 3's heuristic is now load-bearing rather than decorative — unlike dots,
  where the capped path turned out to be value-flat, here it decides 70% of the
  game. Its eval deserves real tests.
- The `Level` names are settled: Easy / Medium / Hard / **Expert**.

## Open Questions

1. **Board size — 6 × 4, or small enough to solve outright?** Recommendation:
   **6 × 4**, the recognizable game, with the honest-Oracle shape the shelf already
   has two precedents for. The cost is a tutor that hedges outside the endgame.
   Phase 0 measures the knee either way, so this can be answered after Phase 0
   rather than before it — but it must be answered before Phase 1 writes a vector.
2. **The name.** "Mancala" is a generic family name and carries no trademark risk,
   so it is safe. But it names a *family*, not this game, and every other rebuilt
   game here has its own name. Recommendation: **Furrow** — a crofting word, which
   is the shelf's own idiom, and it describes the board. Alternatives: Sow, Seed,
   Harvest. ("Kalah" is avoided deliberately: it was a commercial trademark.)
3. **Scope — the full §10 checklist?** Dots took it (tutor, hybrid, harness,
   baselines). Recommendation: yes, minus nothing; the marginal cost per game is
   now small and the hybrid is where the persona roster thread lives.
4. **Two or four players?** The discovery catalog lists mancala's cousins as 2-to-4
   in places. Recommendation: **two only**. The `Adversary` trait is two-sided by
   construction, and a four-player variant is a different plan, not a setting.
